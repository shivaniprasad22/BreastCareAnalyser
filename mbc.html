Do males have breast cancer?


Yes, males can develop breast cancer. Although breast cancer is more common in women, men can also develop the disease. In fact, according to the American Cancer Society, about 2,650 new cases of invasive breast cancer are expected to be diagnosed in men in the United States in 2021.

Men have breast tissue, although it is less developed than in women, and breast cancer can occur when the cells in the breast tissue grow and divide uncontrollably. Risk factors for male breast cancer include age, family history, radiation exposure, hormonal imbalances, and certain genetic mutations.

The symptoms of breast cancer in men are similar to those in women, including a lump in the breast or underarm area, nipple discharge, nipple inversion, and skin changes. Men are encouraged to perform regular self-exams and to seek medical attention if they notice any unusual changes.

Treatment for male breast cancer is similar to that for women and may include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, hormone therapy, or targeted therapy. The choice of treatment depends on the stage and type of cancer, as well as the patient's overall health.

It is important for men to be aware that breast cancer can occur in males and to seek medical attention if they notice any unusual changes in their breasts.


There is no sure way to prevent male breast cancer, since the cause of most breast cancers is not known. However, there are some things you can do to lower your risk of developing it. According to a web search result, some of these are:

- Get to and stay at a healthy weight. Being overweight or obese can increase your risk of breast cancer and other types of cancer. You can maintain     a healthy weight by balancing your food intake with physical activity.
- Avoid or limit alcohol. Alcohol use can increase your risk of breast cancer and other types of cancer. It is best not to drink alcohol, but if you do,
  limit yourself to no more than two drinks per day.
- Be physically active. Physical activity can lower your risk of breast cancer and other types of cancer by helping you control your weight, improve
  your immune system and reduce inflammation. The American Cancer Society recommends that adults get at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate     intensity or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous intensity activity each week (or a combination of these), preferably spread throughout the week.
- Eat a healthy diet. A diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins and healthy fats can help you prevent chronic diseases and lower
 your risk of cancer. Some foods that may have anti-cancer properties include blueberries, pomegranates, broccoli, garlic and turmeric.
- Know your family history. If you have a family history of breast cancer or genetic mutations that increase your risk (such as BRCA1 or BRCA2), you       may want to talk to your doctor about genetic testing or screening options for early detection.

Early detection and treatment can improve your chances of survival and quality of life if you do develop breast cancer. You should be aware of any changes or symptoms in your breasts and see your doctor as soon as possible if you notice anything unusual.
