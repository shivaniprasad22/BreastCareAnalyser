What Is Breast Cancer?



Breast cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the cells of the breast. In breast cancer, abnormal cells in the breast grow and divide uncontrollably, forming a mass or lump. Breast cancer can occur in both men and women, but it is much more common in women. There are different types of breast cancer, including ductal carcinoma, lobular carcinoma, and inflammatory breast cancer. The most common type of breast cancer is ductal carcinoma, which starts in the cells that line the milk ducts. Breast cancer can spread to other parts of the body if not detected and treated early. It is important for women to do regular self-exams and have regular mammograms to detect any changes in their breasts. Treatment for breast cancer may include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or a combination of these. The choice of treatment depends on the type and stage of the cancer, as well as the patient's overall health.

Breast cancer is very common worldwide. According to a web search result², breast cancer became the most commonly diagnosed cancer type in the world in 2020. There were more than 2.26 million new cases of breast cancer and almost 685 000 deaths from breast cancer globally¹². Breast cancer was also the most common cause of cancer death in women and the fifth most common cause of cancer death overall². The incidence and mortality rates of breast cancer vary by region, country and population. Some factors that may influence these rates include: - The availability and accessibility of early detection programmes - The quality and coverage of treatment services - The prevalence of risk factors such as obesity, alcohol consumption and hormone use - The genetic makeup and family history of the population


Prevention Is Better Than Cure


While there is no guaranteed way to prevent breast cancer, there are some steps that people can take to reduce their risk of developing the disease. These include:

- Maintaining a healthy weight.
- Exercising regularly.
- Limiting alcohol consumption.
- Not smoking.
- Breastfeeding (which may reduce the risk of breast cancer).
- Performing regular breast self-exams and attending routine check-ups with a healthcare professional.


Conclusion:


Breast cancer is a serious disease that affects a significant number of women (and men) worldwide. While the exact causes of breast cancer are not fully understood, there are certain factors that may increase a person's risk of developing the disease. If caught early, breast cancer is highly treatable, and there are several treatment options available. It is important for women to perform regular breast self-exams and attend routine check-ups with a healthcare professional to detect any changes in their breasts.
