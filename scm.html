Symptoms and Causes


Symptoms of Male Breast Cancer:


The symptoms of male breast cancer are similar to those in women and may include:

- A lump or thickening in the breast or underarm area
- Nipple discharge (other than breast milk)
- Nipple inversion (when the nipple turns inward)
- Skin changes (such as redness, scaliness, or dimpling)

It is essential to note that not all lumps or changes in the breast are cancerous. However, any unusual changes in the breast should be examined by a healthcare professional.


Causes of Male Breast Cancer:


The exact cause of male breast cancer is unknown, but certain risk factors may increase a person's likelihood of developing the disease. These risk factors include:

- Age: Breast cancer in men is more common in older men, with the average age of diagnosis being 68 years old.
- Family history: If a close relative (such as a mother, sister, or daughter) has had breast cancer, a person's risk of developing the disease is higher.
- Radiation exposure: Men who receive radiation treatment in chest area, such as for lymphoma, may have an increased risk of breast cancer.
- Hormonal imbalances: Men with conditions that affect their hormone levels, such as Klinefelter syndrome, are at an increased risk of breast cancer.
- Certain genetic mutations: Mutations in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes can increase the risk of breast cancer in both men and women.

It is important for men to be aware that breast cancer can occur in males and to seek medical attention if they notice any unusual changes in their breasts.
