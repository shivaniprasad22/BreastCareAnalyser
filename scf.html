Symptoms And Causes


Symptoms of Breast Cancer:


Breast cancer may not always cause symptoms in its early stages. However, some of the common symptoms of breast cancer include:

- A lump or thickening in the breast or underarm area.
- A change in the size or shape of the breast.
- Nipple discharge (other than breast milk).
- Nipple inversion (when the nipple turns inward).
- Skin changes (such as redness, scaliness, or dimpling).
- Breast pain (although this is not a common symptom of breast cancer).

It is essential to note that not all lumps or changes in the breast are cancerous. Still, any unusual changes should be examined by a healthcare professional.


Causes of Breast Cancer:


Breast cancer occurs when there is a mutation or abnormal change in the DNA of breast cells, causing them to grow and divide uncontrollably. While the exact cause of this DNA mutation is unknown, certain risk factors may increase a person's likelihood of developing breast cancer. These risk factors include:

- Age: Breast cancer is more common in women over 50 years of age.
- Gender: Women are more likely to develop breast cancer than men.
- Family history: If a close relative (such as a mother, sister, or daughter) has had breast cancer, a person's risk of developing the disease is higher.
- Genetic mutations: Certain genetic mutations (such as BRCA1 and BRCA2) can increase the risk of breast cancer.
- Hormonal factors: Women who have undergone hormonal treatments (such as hormone replacement therapy) or who have started menstruating      at an early age or reached menopause at a later age are at a higher risk of developing breast cancer.
- Lifestyle factors: Factors such as obesity, alcohol consumption, and lack of physical activity may also increase the risk of breast cancer.
